## Legal Status & Licensing  
Deterministic Signing Episodes for Distributed Trust

## 1. Project Legal Status  
The project is developed and maintained by a single founder who holds centralized responsibility for the codebase, architecture, and documentation.  
This structure ensures coherent decision‑making, consistent execution, and clear ownership across all phases.

> “The project is currently developed and maintained by the project founder, who holds centralized responsibility and control over the codebase, technical architecture, and documentation.”

This centralized model is intentional and supports high‑assurance development.

---

## 2. Intellectual Property Ownership  
All intellectual property created during Phase I and Phase II is owned by the project founder or a designated legal entity.  
This includes:

- source code  
- architecture specifications  
- protocol definitions  
- documentation  
- verification artifacts  

> “All intellectual property created during Phase I and Phase II is owned by the project founder or a designated legal entity.”

Clear IP ownership ensures continuity and long‑term stewardship.

---

## 3. Licensing Model  
The project adopts a flexible licensing strategy.  
It is designed to support:

- open‑source transparency  
- external auditability  
- potential dual‑licensing  
- controlled commercial use  

No final license is committed yet, allowing alignment with future funding, partnerships, or standardization efforts.

> “The project intends to adopt a flexible licensing approach, such as an open-source or dual-licensing model.”

This flexibility ensures the project can evolve without legal constraints.

---

## 4. Rights of Funding Bodies  
Funding bodies may receive rights consistent with program requirements, including:

- review of deliverables  
- audit of technical results  
- use of outputs for evaluation or research  

However, **no transfer of core intellectual property occurs** unless explicitly agreed in writing.

> “Funding bodies may receive rights… No transfer of core intellectual property occurs unless explicitly agreed in writing.”

This protects the integrity and independence of the project.

---

## 5. Compliance & Transparency  
The project is designed to comply with common public funding requirements:

- openness  
- auditability  
- traceability of results  
- responsible IP management  

All execution paths are deterministic and independently verifiable, supporting transparent review.

> “The project is designed to comply with common public funding requirements, including openness, auditability, traceability of results, and responsible intellectual property management.”

This ensures the system is suitable for high‑assurance environments.

---

## 6. Determinism as a Legal Guarantee  
Determinism is not only a technical property — it is a legal and compliance guarantee.

Because:

- execution is reproducible  
- audit records are explicit  
- no hidden state exists  
- no randomness influences results  

External reviewers can independently verify correctness without relying on trust.

This aligns with legal expectations for transparency and accountability.

---

## 7. Long‑Term Stewardship  
The legal structure ensures:

- continuity of development  
- stable ownership  
- clear responsibility  
- predictable governance  
- compatibility with future collaboration  

Phase III may involve external reviewers or standardization bodies, but ownership remains centralized unless formally transferred.

---

## 8. Conclusion  
The legal status and licensing model provide a stable foundation for responsible development, transparent auditability, and long‑term sustainability.  
Clear IP ownership, flexible licensing, and compliance with public funding requirements ensure that the project remains trustworthy, adaptable, and aligned with high‑assurance environments.

> “The legal status and licensing structure support responsible funding, clear IP ownership, and sustainable long-term development.”
